RA6M5 probe board exploration

Bringing up a SparkFun Thing Plus RA6M5 as a smart telemetry probe for an M.2 Hailo-8: rail power, airflow, a PWM fan with tachometer and a thermocouple input, then using it to find out how much cooling the card needs.

16–19 Nov 2026Host: AMD Strix HaloCard: Hailo-8 M.2, ResNet-50, async depth 4Firmware: mb-probe-ra6m5 0.4.0

Key findings

104 °C

Hailo-8 die with the fan off under sustained load. With the fan at 100 % it settles at 48 °C.

−12 % fps

Thermal throttle at 103.7 °C: the core clock dropped from 400 to 350 MHz and frame rate from about 1190 to 1045 fps.

+25 % power

The same 1190 fps cost 4.42 W at 103 °C against 3.53 W at 48 °C. A hot die burns more power for the same work.

337 → 267 fps/W

Efficiency at 48 °C against 103 °C: cooling the card is worth 26 % more frames per watt.

~25 % duty

Roughly where the fan starts to matter: at 20 % duty the card stopped throttling, and from 25 % the die cooled quickly.

What was built

The board owns its sensors, samples them far faster than the GUI's 1 Hz tick, and reports per-second mean, minimum and maximum as plain-text lines over USB. The host only parses. Details are in docs/ra6m5-probe.md.

  • In the app: fan speed and duty are drawn on the Airflow graph for now, scaled to share its axis (rpm ÷ 1000, duty ÷ 10). Telemetry → Fan sets the duty, starts from whatever the board reports so opening the app never stops a running fan, and logs every change.
  • Flashing takes no button press: tools/flash-ra6m5.sh builds and uploads. Two boards exist, both mapped to the Hailo by USB serial number.
  • Power readings agree with the card's own sensor. The INA228 reads 1.7 % above the Hailo-8's on-board INA231 at idle and 3.3 % above it under load, and voltage × current matches the power register to within 0.0002 %.

The cooling session, 19 Nov

About 34 minutes of ResNet-50 on the Hailo-8 while the fan duty was changed by hand. Efficiency is the app's own fps/W: frame rate divided by the INA228 rail power. Two long stretches with the fan off let the die heat until it throttled; a final sweep from 0 to 100 % in roughly 5 % steps shows how quickly each setting pulls it back. Hover or tap any panel to read every value at that moment.

Move over the charts to read values

The first two minutes were a quick check: fan stepped to 100 %, a short run, the fan switched off and back on. The long run starts at 10:46:37.

Throttling

The Hailo-8 throttled only once, and only after its die sat above 103 °C for more than a minute. On the second fan-off stretch, the clock went from 400 to 350 MHz at 11:03:38 with the die at 103.7 °C, and the frame rate fell by the same 12.5 %. It came back at 11:07:06, once the fan at 20 % duty had brought the die down to 99 °C. On the first fan-off stretch the die reached 101.6 °C and the fan went back on just before the throttle point.

Hot silicon costs power

Every one-second sample where the card ran at full clock and 1165–1215 fps. The work is the same at every point, yet the rail power climbs steadily with die temperature, from 3.4 W near 35 °C to 4.4 W above 100 °C. Any fps/W figure for this card depends on how well it is cooled.

INA228 rail power at constant ~1190 fps, against Hailo-8 die temperature

Power efficiency

Frames per watt for every one-second sample of the run, against die temperature. At full clock the frame rate stays near 1190 fps, so efficiency falls only because power rises: from about 343 fps/W with a cool die (37 °C) to 267 fps/W above 103 °C. Throttling does not recover it. At 350 MHz the card drew less power but lost frames in proportion, landing at 264 fps/W.

The fan

Noctua NF-A4x10 5V PWM. Speed and airflow are the mean of the last 10 seconds at each setting. The duty column is the value the EMC2101 actually applied (it has 64 steps, so a 20 % request reads back as 20.6 %).

Fan speed (rpm) against duty (%)
Airflow (m/s) against duty (%)
  • The fan starts at about 10 % duty (440 rpm), but the airflow sensor reads almost nothing below about 15 %.
  • Speed rises in a straight line up to 70 % duty (about 65 rpm per percent), then flattens at about 4 750 rpm. Settings above 75 % buy almost nothing.
  • Top speed crept up through the session, from 4 110 rpm on the first spin-up to about 4 750 rpm by the end, probably as the bearings warmed. Earlier spot checks (4 190–4 380 rpm) were taken before it had settled.

Lessons from the bench

  • Re-plugging the sensor chain needs a rescan. The firmware looks for sensors only at power-up, so sensors connected later stay absent until the host sends rescan.
  • Restart the app after re-flashing or unplugging the board. The app does not reconnect, so readings go blank three seconds after the board drops off.

Still open

  • Fit the thermocouple. The MAX31855K is detected and reads its own die temperature, but it reports an open circuit because no thermocouple is connected, so its column is empty in this log.
  • A second airflow sensor, if needed. The FS3000's address is fixed and the board has one I2C bus, so a second sensor would need an I2C mux (TCA9548A).
  • The per-second minimum and maximum that the board sends are not used by the app yet.
  • Possible app work: a fan graph of its own, and a fan duty that automation plans can set for each step.
